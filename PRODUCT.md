# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Prospective clients evaluating Dan Brandt's product-development work, and
people who need authoritative support or privacy information for a published
Dan Brandt app.

## Product Purpose

dbdoo.dev is Dan Brandt's public portfolio and durable support surface. It
shows what Dan builds, how he works, and provides trustworthy public
documentation for released products.

## Positioning

The site pairs unusually fast, AI-assisted implementation with repeated
hands-on product and UX refinement. It demonstrates finished work and gives
visitors a direct path to contact the person who builds it.

## Operating Context

Visitors arrive from referrals, product listings, app-store disclosures, and
project links. Public policy pages must remain available without signing in,
work on mobile, and provide a stable HTTPS URL suitable for Google Play.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The public domain is `dbdoo.dev` and the site is deployed through Vercel.
- Product support currently uses `dbdoo.dev@gmail.com`.
- Product and privacy claims must match the exact shipped implementation.
- Legal and policy pages prioritize durable URLs, clear headings, plain
  language, accessible links, and visible effective dates.
- Commercial claims, customer evidence, prices, and data practices must never
  be invented.

## Brand Commitments

The public identity is Dan Brandt / dbdoo.dev. Preserve the existing dark,
direct, product-focused portfolio identity. Released dot.o products use the
`dot.o` name and a precise dot-based visual language.

## Evidence on Hand

- Existing portfolio implementation under `src/app`.
- Live deployment at `https://dbdoo.dev`.
- Implementation-backed dot.o Launcher release inventory maintained in its
  source repository.

## Product Principles

- Make the visitor's next action obvious.
- Prove claims with real work and implementation-backed facts.
- Keep policy and support material plain, complete, and easy to scan.
- Preserve stable public URLs once used in store listings.
- Treat accessibility and mobile readability as release requirements.
